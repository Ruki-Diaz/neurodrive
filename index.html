HOW NEURODRIVE works

Explore the five-station autonomous pipeline in 3D. Switch between Assembled, Cutaway, Stations, and One drive, or click any station to inspect its role.

01

Map · Real OpenStreetMap Streets

OSM streets

Real OpenStreetMap geometries of the Melbourne CBD (Hoddle Grid) are projected into metric coordinates (+x east, +y south). Road widths, speed limits (40 km/h), one-way Little streets, building footprints, and tram tracks are indexed into a spatial hash grid.

02

Router · A* Graph Pathfinder

15 m waypoints

Road polylines split at intersections to form a directed graph honouring one-way streets. A* computes the optimal route—such as Flinders St Station → Queen Victoria Market—placing target waypoints every 15 m.

03

Sensors · 7-Ray Lidar & Signals

7 rays

Each 4.5 m × 1.9 m vehicle casts 7 forward rays against road edges, traffic cars, and trams, combining proximity readings with current speed, sin/cos bearing to the next waypoint, and the distance and phase of the next traffic signal.

04

Brain · 12–14–2 Neural Network

12-14-2 net

Twelve normalized telemetry inputs feed into a [12, 14, 2] feed-forward network with tanh activations. Two output neurons continuously control bicycle-model steering and acceleration/braking.

05

Evolution · Genetic Selection

next gen

Drivers are scored by metres progressed along the A* route plus arrival bonuses and traffic-rule penalties. The best drivers breed and mutate the next generation, auto-advancing scenarios at ≥70% arrival success.
